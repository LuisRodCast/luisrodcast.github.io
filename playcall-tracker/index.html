Shiny Application · In production

Every possession, tracked. Every decision, grounded in data.

A mobile-first R Shiny application the coaching staff opens on an iPad during practice and games, taps once per possession to log the play, result, ball reversals, paint touches, clock, quarter, and category — then flips to a dashboard that answers the questions coaches actually ask: which plays travel from practice to games, which categories produce the points, what the ACWR curvature says about readiness.

The question

Coaches make dozens of play calls per game. Most of them get evaluated by feel afterward, which is where good evaluations get buried under confirmation bias and the vividness of the last possession. If an assistant is going to log every possession anyway, the data should answer actual coaching questions — not just produce a spreadsheet nobody reads.

The data

One row per possession, logged in < 3 seconds on an iPad from the bench. Fifteen possible result tiles covering every realistic outcome: made/missed field goals, 1-, 2-, and 3-shot free-throw sequences, And1 conversions (2 + 1 and 3 + 1), turnovers, non-shooting fouls, and empty possessions. Alongside the result: play name from the team's playbook, quarter, optional category (Transition / Secondary / 2nd Shots / HC Sets for games), per-possession ball reversals, paint touches, and clock. Debounced auto-save to a Turso cloud SQLite backend means the staff never thinks about saving.

The approach

Mobile-first by necessity. Built with shinyMobile on a Framework7 dark theme, scaled for a 12.9" iPad in landscape. Big tap targets. One-handed operation. Dark mode so the glow doesn't announce itself to the arena. Session type (Practice vs Game) is a top-level toggle that changes which quarters are available and whether category tagging appears.

Statistical rigor where it matters. The Trends tab doesn't just show numbers — it shows whether the numbers mean anything. The practice-vs-game comparison runs a Welch's t-test per play on the raw possession points vectors, reports Δ PPP with a 95% confidence interval, and colors the delta pill based on whether the CI crosses zero (not just the point estimate's sign). A play with a +0.5 PPP delta from 3 practice reps and 2 game reps gets a gray "too close to call" pill, not a misleading green one.

Production backend, isolated per-visitor demo. The production app persists to Turso cloud SQLite over HTTP, with every save a debounced batched write. The public demo linked below swaps that backend for an in-memory per-visitor R list so every browser tab stays isolated — no credentials, no shared data, no risk to the production database.

What's inside

  • TrackerLive possession logger. Play dropdown, category dropdown (games), quarter chips, 15-tile result grid, ball-reversal and paint-touch counters, play history underneath
  • SummaryPer-play rollup for the current session. In game mode, expands to one row per (play × category) tuple so you can see which plays work in which contexts
  • CoachLast-5 strip, 8 top-line stat tiles (POSS, PPP, FG%, TURNOVERS, TO%, LAST 5 PPP…), Best/Worst/Most-Used hero cards, play comparison table, category filter chips
  • SheetChronological play-by-play log, with CSV export for post-game review
  • HistorySaved sessions browser. View/Delete, with per-session rollup stats
  • TrendsCross-session analytics: PPP over sessions, usage frequency, make/miss/TO mix, quarter heatmap, plus a CATEGORY ANALYSES block (usage share, PPP, play × category heatmap, category × quarter heatmap, ball-reversals and paint-touches tactical-signature scatters) and a PRACTICE vs GAME table with Welch's t-test + 95% CI

The live demo is a sanitized build of the production app — same UI, same analytics, same statistical pipeline, but with a generic playbook (ISO, HORNS, FLEX, PNR-L/R, DRAG, etc.), two preloaded fake sessions so the app lands with data to click through, and no connection to any real database. Nothing you log during the demo persists beyond your browser tab.
